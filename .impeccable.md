# OHM Map — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Primary: the **history enthusiast** — a curious adult who reads pop-history, browses Wikipedia rabbit holes, plays Crusader Kings, watches documentaries. Not an academic, not a casual visitor: someone who *wants* atmosphere and is willing to spend 20–40 minutes drifting through a map of the past.

Context: late-evening exploration sessions on a laptop or tablet, usually after dinner. Often arriving via a shared link from a friend or a forum. Patient. Will reward depth.

Job to be done: *"Show me what the world looked like in [year] and let me wander."* Discovery, not analytics. The interface should invite drift, not optimize click-paths.

### Brand Personality
Three words: **scholarly + archival + considered.**

- Voice: writes like a museum exhibit caption — confident, factual, occasionally dry, never breathless. No exclamation marks. No "🔥 amazing".
- Emotional goal: the calm of a well-lit reading room at 10pm. Hushed. Time slows down. The user feels they've stumbled into something that was made carefully.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Dark — library at night.** Warm-near-black ground, ivory body text, a single brass / aged-gold accent for live interaction. No secondary color. No gradients.

Spirit references (allowed inspiration, NOT to be cloned):
- The deckle-edged plates in a 19th-century historical atlas.
- The brass year-dial on an antique astronomical clock.
- The reading room of a university library at 22:30.
- A David Rumsey scan, viewed full-screen at night.

Anti-references (must not look like any of these):
- The current Angular Material indigo-pink default — admin-app, not history-site.
- Web3 / cyberpunk: cyan-on-dark, purple→blue gradients, glowing accents. Wrong genre entirely.
- SaaS dashboard kit: card grids, sidebar navs, KPI tiles, dashboard chrome.

The map is the hero. Chrome recedes to the edges. The map should look like the artifact; the UI should look like the matte around it.

### Design Principles

1. **The map is sovereign.** Chrome lives in the margins. No floating panels hovering over the map. No drop shadows on the map surface. The map fills.
2. **Time is a tactile object.** The timeline ruler is the spine of the experience and gets the most craft. It should look engraved — typeset year labels, a weighted cursor, hairline tick marks. Not a default vis-timeline.
3. **Quiet contrast.** Warm-near-black ground, ivory text, ONE brass accent reserved for the live cursor / active state / hovered link. Use it sparingly so it carries weight.
4. **Editorial, not templated.** Asymmetry where it earns its place. No card grids anywhere. The right-side sidenavs feel like inserted plates in an atlas — flat, slim, with a deckle-edged inner border, not a nav drawer.
5. **Restraint everywhere except the moment of state change.** When the year advances, the world changes — that transition earns motion. Everything else is still. No idle micro-animations.

### Type direction (not yet chosen — to be picked at craft time)
Pair a humanist or transitional serif for display + a refined sans or quiet serif for UI. **Banned**: Inter, DM Sans, Fraunces, Crimson, Playfair, Cormorant, IBM Plex *, Space Grotesk, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument *, Newsreader, Lora, Syne. Look further — Pangram Pangram, Velvetyne, Klim, Adobe Fonts catalogues. Aim for type that could plausibly have been used in a 1962 Phaidon art-history monograph.

### Color direction (to be locked at craft time)
Working in OKLCH. Approximate targets:
- Ground: warm-near-black around `oklch(0.18 0.012 60)` (NOT pure black; tinted toward umber).
- Body ivory: around `oklch(0.92 0.015 80)`.
- Brass accent (one only): around `oklch(0.74 0.12 75)` — to be tuned against the actual map style at lock-in.
- Hairlines: a tinted neutral at around `oklch(0.32 0.01 60)`.
